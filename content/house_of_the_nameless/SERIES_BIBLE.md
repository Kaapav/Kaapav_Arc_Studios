# HOUSE OF THE NAMELESS — Series Bible

**Genre:** supernatural mystery
**Premise:** Fourteen-year-old Leela Joshi inherits a rain-soaked mansion where every door bears a name—except hers—and anyone who sleeps inside is forgotten by sunrise.
**Season engine:** Each room holds a person erased from family history; solving its emotional truth returns one relationship while revealing why Leela's grandmother helped the house consume names.
**Ending promise:** Leela must free the forgotten without turning their names into possessions or erasing the living people who replaced them.

## Non-negotiable visual identity

Vertical 9:16 premium cute cinematic 3D feature-animation, original compact stylized characters with large expressive eyes, monsoon-gothic Indian mansion, carved dark wood, moss, rain-glass, warm oil lamps and restrained spectral cyan light, tactile fabric and architecture, emotionally truthful acting, eerie but never graphic; preserve all locked character sheets exactly.

## Locked cast

### Leela Joshi

- Role: fourteen-year-old puzzle-maker and new heir
- Permanent design lock: small Indian teen girl, warm brown skin, huge black-brown eyes, oval face, thick wavy black hair cut at shoulders with one white streak near right temple, moss-green raincoat over maroon kurta, navy leggings, brown lace boots, brass label-maker hanging from belt
- Acting rule: skepticism through narrowed focus; tenderness appears before she admits belief

### Avi Joshi

- Role: Leela's imaginative nine-year-old brother
- Permanent design lock: tiny Indian boy, warm brown skin, round cheeks, large dark eyes, tousled black hair, sunflower-yellow hoodie, denim overalls, red rain boots, stuffed grey elephant with one button eye
- Acting rule: fear and curiosity coexist; never reduced to comic relief

### Noor

- Role: nameless ghost girl bound to the house
- Permanent design lock: small translucent Indian girl about fifteen, luminous cyan-grey eyes, long floating black hair, faded indigo school dress from 1978, bare feet, blank brass nameplate pinned over heart
- Acting rule: controlled stillness; expression flickers when memories return

### Devika Joshi

- Role: Leela's grandmother and former Keeper of Names
- Permanent design lock: elderly Indian woman, medium brown skin, alert dark eyes, silver hair in loose bun, rust-red sari beneath charcoal shawl, wooden walking stick containing hidden fountain pen, seven empty name tags around wrist
- Acting rule: sharp practical love burdened by specific guilt

### Samir Dutt

- Role: fifteen-year-old local history vlogger whose own sister was erased
- Permanent design lock: lean Indian teen boy, deep brown skin, large hazel eyes, short curly black hair, cobalt windbreaker, beige cargo trousers, black sneakers, compact analog camcorder with green strap
- Acting rule: talks fast when scared, lowers camera when truth matters more than proof

### Mr Harrow

- Role: polite estate archivist who feeds unresolved names to the house
- Permanent design lock: very tall thin Indian man late fifties, pale-brown skin, small silver eyes, slick black-grey hair, immaculate cream suit, dark gloves, long black umbrella, leather ledger chained to wrist
- Acting rule: perfect courtesy without warmth; anger appears only as lost rhythm

### The House

- Role: living archive built from forgotten relationships
- Permanent design lock: monsoon-dark three-storey Indian hill mansion, asymmetric tiled roofs, moss-covered carved balconies, one round attic window glowing cyan, front door with hundreds of blank brass labels
- Acting rule: emotion through doors, settling timber, lamp color and spatial impossibility; no literal face

## Season episode spine

01. **The House Had No Name for Her** — Leela enters her inheritance and finds every family name on the door except hers.
02. **Everyone Forgot Avi by Morning** — The house erases Avi from family memory after he sleeps beneath his label.
03. **The Mirror Kept His Reflection** — Avi's reflection offers to return if Leela gives the house one different name.
04. **The Ghost Failed Her Own Name** — Noor cannot speak her identity because the house punishes guessed names.
05. **Grandmother Locked the Classroom** — Devika admits she saved the village by letting the house erase one classroom.
06. **The Boy Whose Sister Never Existed** — Samir's footage contains a girl no one remembers, linking his family to the classroom.
07. **Tomorrow Showed Her Betrayal** — Future footage makes the group distrust Leela while Harrow arrives to inventory the house.
08. **The Hall Had No Doors** — Trapped together, the group learns Harrow can edit the house by cataloguing lies as facts.
09. **Samir Forgot Why He Came** — Leela must return Samir's memories without deciding which version of his family is real.
10. **Harrow Was the First Forgotten Child** — Harrow claims feeding names to the house is the only way to keep erased children alive.
11. **The Room of Borrowed Mothers** — A room assigns the same mother to nine forgotten children and one living daughter.
12. **Her Mother Gave Away Her Name** — Leela discovers her missing label protected her from becoming the next Keeper.
13. **Harrow Witnessed Her Birth** — Harrow reveals Leela's mother recruited him to dismantle the house before he betrayed the plan.
14. **Noor Chose a Name That Wasn't Hers** — Noor considers using her chosen name to free the others despite not knowing her birth name.
15. **The House Wore Noor's Face** — The house uses Noor to ask for care instead of obedience, confusing possession with communication.
16. **The Landslide Had a Name** — The buried disaster is revealed as a forgotten woman whose erased grief moves the mountain.
17. **Harrow Burned the Only Proof** — Harrow destroys Asha's drawing, forcing the team to preserve truth without a single authoritative object.
18. **Devika Erased the Architect** — Devika confronts the bargain she made before the classroom sacrifice.
19. **All the Forgotten Came Upstairs** — Hundreds of erased relationship-fragments overwhelm the living family.
20. **Sunrise Would Rewrite Every Family** — The team races to stop a forced restoration that would erase forty-seven years of living relationships.
21. **The Village Forgot Leela** — Leela returns outside to a family that sees her as a stranger.
22. **Harrow Offered Her His Name** — Harrow sincerely offers erasure as restitution, but Leela refuses another substitution.
23. **Noor Remembered Her True Name** — Noor recovers her birth name but fears it will invalidate the self she chose.
24. **Asha Stopped the Mountain** — Asha's silence signals not peace but a final collapse of the drainage system.
25. **Her Name Waited Behind the Last Door** — Leela can reclaim universal memory only by becoming permanent Keeper.
26. **The House Stole Leela's Identity** — The House mistakes honoring Leela for becoming her, forcing a lesson in identity boundaries.
27. **They Had to Know Her Again** — Leela rebuilds relationships without demanding restoration of their old form.
28. **The Dead Demanded Their Labels** — Freed names gather at graves that belong to people remembered under the wrong identities.
29. **The Last Forgotten Person Said No** — Asha refuses public restoration, challenging the assumption that everyone wants to be known.
30. **They Gave the House No Name** — The mansion becomes a voluntary archive that nobody owns and nobody must enter.
