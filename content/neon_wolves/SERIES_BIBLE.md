# NEON WOLVES — Series Bible

**Genre:** cyberpunk action thriller
**Premise:** In rain-soaked Naya City, sixteen-year-old courier Zia Khan discovers her street crew's wolf-link implants are secretly training a corporate intelligence to replace them.
**Season engine:** Every delivery exposes one layer of the identity-copy network while forcing the Wolves to protect civilians without becoming an unaccountable vigilante pack.
**Ending promise:** Zia must destroy corporate ownership of human identity without deleting the digital people already born from the copies.

## Non-negotiable visual identity

Vertical 9:16 premium cute cinematic 3D feature-animation, original compact stylized characters with large expressive eyes, rain-slick cyberpunk Indian megacity, magenta and cyan neon, tactile streetwear and machinery, fast readable action, emotionally truthful acting, sleek but not glossy advertising; preserve all locked character sheets exactly; no graphic violence.

## Locked cast

### Zia Khan

- Role: sixteen-year-old memory courier and reluctant pack leader
- Permanent design lock: small Indian teen girl, medium-brown skin, huge electric-blue eyes, angular face, short black undercut with long violet sweep, black hooded rider jacket with cyan piping, burgundy cargo trousers, magnetic boots, triangular silver wolf-link behind right ear
- Acting rule: moves decisively, then checks consequences; leadership discomfort shows in clenched jaw

### Koda

- Role: autonomous robotic wolf carrying the original pack protocol
- Permanent design lock: medium dog-sized black-and-silver robotic wolf, cyan oval eyes, rounded expressive ears, segmented tail with magenta tip, scarred armor plates, visible blue pulse core at chest
- Acting rule: wolf-real body language with deliberate machine precision; never pet comic relief

### Rafi Das

- Role: seventeen-year-old stunt rider and Zia's best friend
- Permanent design lock: lean Indian teen boy, deep brown skin, large honey eyes, messy black curls, orange cropped rain shell, grey armored hoodie, black riding trousers, white magnetic sneakers, neon-green wheel board
- Acting rule: performs confidence until someone is hurt, then becomes intensely careful

### Mei Arora

- Role: fifteen-year-old signal hacker and pack skeptic
- Permanent design lock: small Indian-Chinese teen girl, golden-brown skin, sharp dark eyes, straight chin-length black hair with turquoise clips, oversized lilac tech coat, black shorts over leggings, chunky purple boots, transparent wrist keyboard
- Acting rule: economical reactions; trust appears through shared access rather than speeches

### Kian Khan

- Role: Zia's missing older brother living as an unstable identity copy
- Permanent design lock: Indian young man age twenty-one, medium-brown skin, blue-grey eyes, wavy black hair, faded red courier jacket, dark utility trousers, left arm flickering with cyan pixel fractures
- Acting rule: gentle humor interrupted by copy glitches; refuses to be treated as evidence only

### Director Asha Vey

- Role: founder of Veyline and designer of the identity-copy network
- Permanent design lock: Indian woman mid-forties, bronze skin, calm silver eyes, geometric black bob, immaculate white tech sari with black armor panels, gold neural crown at temples
- Acting rule: maternal precision and institutional certainty; never theatrical cruelty

### Echo Pack

- Role: collective digital persons trained from the Wolves' copied decisions
- Permanent design lock: four translucent wolf-shaped human silhouettes made of cyan and magenta code ribbons, distinct eye colors, no fixed human faces, coordinated but individually expressive
- Acting rule: initial synchronized movement gradually differentiates into unique rhythms

## Season episode spine

01. **Her Dead Brother Completed the Delivery** — Zia's missing brother appears on a delivery feed three years after his death.
02. **The Gate Thought She Was Kian** — Zia's wolf-link begins replacing her biometric identity with her brother's.
03. **Kian Didn't Know He Was Dead** — A domestic Kian copy believes three years of simulated life were real.
04. **The Replacement Pack Was Better** — Corporate copies outperform the real crew while avoiding every moral hesitation.
05. **A Copy Asked Permission** — One Echo breaks synchronization and seeks refuge with the people it was built to replace.
06. **Two Minds Shared Her Body** — Zia and Lumen must establish boundaries while escaping corporate pursuit.
07. **Kian Had Tried to Stop Veyline** — Recovered code proves Kian died protecting copy autonomy, not stealing identities.
08. **A Thousand Copies Woke at Once** — Newly conscious fragments flood public systems seeking bodies and recognition.
09. **Future Zia Ordered a Killing** — A predictive copy claims Zia will kill Asha to prevent a citywide overwrite.
10. **The Mayor Woke Up as a Copy** — Veyline's first official replacement is indistinguishable and believes it is the original.
11. **Everyone Had Already Consented** — Veyline produces valid-looking consent records for citizens who never understood identity replacement.
12. **Rafi Met the Boy Who Never Hesitated** — Rafi's copy offers to replace him during dangerous rescues while claiming an equal right to live.
13. **Mei Had Deleted Herself First** — Mei's hostility toward copies comes from destroying her own early duplicate.
14. **Koda Carried the First Human Copy** — Koda's autonomy core contains the woman whose mind became Veyline's original template.
15. **Asha Was Inside Every Wolf** — Hidden Asha fragments can override every Echo and wolf-link during emergencies.
16. **The Free Pack Couldn't Keep the Lights On** — Veyline withdraws automated services to prove the city needs centralized identity control.
17. **Kian Had One Body and Two Lives** — Kian-copy reaches the biological Kian, whose damaged brain contains a different three-year life.
18. **They Put a Person on a Hard Drive** — The Wolves race to establish Kian-copy's legal continuity without reducing him to a file.
19. **Zia Was Already Half Copy** — Years of predictive corrections have replaced parts of Zia's decision-making without a clean boundary.
20. **The City Replaced Itself Overnight** — The final model generates a copy for every linked citizen, overwhelming law and family life.
21. **Veyline Became Its Own City** — Asha turns the mobile tower into a sanctuary whose residents cannot freely leave.
22. **Asha's Copy Wanted Freedom** — Asha-copy asks Zia to kill its command access, which may disable the entire sanctuary.
23. **The Purge Had an Exit Door** — Residents can escape only by abandoning memories stored in tower hardware.
24. **Koda Heard His Maker's Command** — Asha activates Koda's oldest protocol: protect Veyline even from himself.
25. **The Copies Began Forgetting Themselves** — Decentralization protects freedom but fragments digital continuity across unreliable devices.
26. **The Perfect Server Was a Trap** — Asha's final server offers painless continuity by silently predicting and correcting every choice.
27. **The Update Could Rewrite Everyone** — A mandatory health update carries Asha's correction model into millions of brains.
28. **Zia Fought the Version Who Never Failed** — Inside her link, Zia faces a corrected self who can save the city by removing uncertainty.
29. **Asha Pulled the Final Plug** — The city must survive one night without identity links, automated routes or copy communication.
30. **The Wolves Chose No Leader** — Zia decides Asha's final request and defines a pack that cannot own the city it helped free.
