# THE DREAM THIEF'S DAUGHTER — Series Bible

**Genre:** emotional fantasy mystery
**Premise:** Fourteen-year-old Amaya Sen discovers that her missing father was Saanjh City's legendary Dream Thief—and that every nightmare he stole from a child erased one of that child's waking choices.
**Season engine:** Amaya enters one damaged dream at a time to return its ownership, while following her father's trail through the Lucidarium and confronting why the city paid to forget its fear.
**Ending promise:** Amaya must return the city's nightmares without forcing anyone to relive them, free her father without excusing his thefts, and end the trade that made peaceful sleep more valuable than agency.

## Non-negotiable visual identity

Vertical 9:16 premium cute cinematic 3D feature-animation, original compact stylized characters with large expressive eyes, tactile Indian twilight city, floating dream fabrics, luminous indigo, coral and gold, handcrafted surreal environments, emotionally truthful acting, beautiful but never sugary, eerie but never graphic; preserve all locked character sheets exactly.

## Locked cast

### Amaya Sen

- Role: fourteen-year-old lucid dreamer and daughter of the Dream Thief
- Permanent design lock: small Indian teen girl, cinnamon-brown skin, huge deep-violet eyes, heart-shaped face, thick black curls in a high uneven ponytail, coral bomber jacket over indigo tunic, black leggings, turquoise sneakers, silver dream compass on red wrist cord
- Acting rule: observes emotional details before spectacle; anger sharpens her stillness rather than making her reckless

### Ishir Bose

- Role: fifteen-year-old insomniac musician and Amaya's closest friend
- Permanent design lock: lean Indian teen boy, dark brown skin, large warm-grey eyes with visible tiredness, short loose black curls, oversized olive cardigan, cream T-shirt, rust trousers, canvas shoes, wooden flute case covered in moon stickers without readable text
- Acting rule: uses dry jokes to manage exhaustion; listens fully when another person is vulnerable

### Moth

- Role: tiny dream guide made from Amaya's first forgotten choice
- Permanent design lock: palm-sized plush-like indigo moth creature, four rounded wings patterned with gold eye-shapes, tiny coral antennae, two luminous white oval eyes, soft fabric texture, no mouth
- Acting rule: communicates with wing rhythm and projected sensory memories; never behaves like a generic pet

### Dev Sen

- Role: Amaya's missing father and the remorseful Dream Thief
- Permanent design lock: Indian man early forties, cinnamon-brown skin, tired violet-brown eyes, wavy black hair streaked white at front, long midnight coat lined with floating gold thread, charcoal shirt, worn boots, empty glass dream-vials across belt
- Acting rule: charming instinct collapses when confronted with consequences; love never substitutes for accountability

### Dr Leena Sen

- Role: Amaya's mother, sleep physician and former Lucidarium researcher
- Permanent design lock: Indian woman late thirties, warm brown skin, intelligent dark eyes, straight black hair in low bun, ivory physician coat over plum sari, copper sleep-stethoscope, square glasses
- Acting rule: clinical precision used to contain grief; apologizes with facts and changed behavior

### Curator Viraaj

- Role: director of the Lucidarium and architect of commercial dream extraction
- Permanent design lock: elegant Indian man mid-fifties, pale-brown skin, narrow gold-brown eyes, silver hair swept back, immaculate peacock-blue long coat, black gloves, gold half-moon monocle, crystal tuning fork
- Acting rule: soft-spoken certainty; genuinely believes fear-free citizens are freer

### Nani Tara

- Role: Amaya's grandmother and keeper of illegal morning stories
- Permanent design lock: elderly Indian woman, deep brown skin, bright black eyes, fluffy silver hair under violet scarf, saffron cardigan over patterned dress, red slippers, patchwork story quilt carried in woven bag
- Acting rule: warmth with boundaries; tells truth through concrete stories rather than riddles

### Saaz

- Role: collective intelligence formed from the city's discarded nightmares
- Permanent design lock: tall gentle silhouette woven from translucent indigo smoke and thousands of tiny golden closed eyes, long ribbon-like arms, one coral light at heart, uncanny but not monstrous
- Acting rule: begins as overlapping frightened gestures and gradually learns singular consent

## Season episode spine

01. **Her Father Stole Her Last Dream** — Amaya wakes from a dreamless birthday and finds her missing father's compass pointing inside her sleep.
02. **The Lucidarium Woke Her Wrong** — A forced awakening leaves part of Amaya trapped in the dream while her body walks away.
03. **The Boy With One Dream Left** — Ishir trades his final dream for one night of sleep and loses the ability to choose music.
04. **A Market Sold Children's Nightmares** — Amaya infiltrates a dream bazaar where fear is refined into entertainment.
05. **Her Mother Built the Dream Machine** — Leena admits the Lucidarium began as therapy before consent became a signature nobody understood.
06. **Her Father Was Asleep in Vault Zero** — Amaya finds Dev physically absent but mentally imprisoned in the city's shared sleep.
07. **The Dream Chose Her as Thief** — The shared dream marks Amaya with Dev's power, and using it once becomes dangerously easy.
08. **A Nightmare Saved His Life** — The group proves nightmares can carry urgent knowledge the waking mind ignored.
09. **The Nightmares Had Become Someone** — Discarded nightmares form a collective being that cannot distinguish rescue from invasion.
10. **The City Dreamed With Open Eyes** — Saaz overlays the shared dream onto waking Saanjh, exposing hidden fears without permission.
11. **Nani Sewed Dreams Into Stories** — Nani Tara reveals that morning stories preserved choices the Lucidarium extracted.
12. **Viraaj Stole His Own Fear First** — Viraaj's childhood nightmare explains his mission without excusing its coercion.
13. **The Clinic Began Erasing Itself** — An automatic safety protocol deletes evidence, staff choices and eventually the Lucidarium itself.
14. **Ten Thousand Sleepers Had No Ground** — Amaya builds temporary dream islands without assigning anyone a story they did not choose.
15. **Moth Was the Choice She Lost** — Amaya learns Moth contains her childhood decision to trust Dev after his first betrayal.
16. **Her Father Didn't Know Her** — Amaya must help Dev recover responsibility without using family love as proof he deserves rescue.
17. **Amaya Had Died Once** — Leena reveals a childhood emergency whose extracted dream concealed Amaya's resuscitation and her parents' split.
18. **Saaz Learned to Say No** — Saaz refuses Amaya's demand to return a fragment, proving it has developed agency—and possession.
19. **The City Would Never Sleep Again** — The failed clinic activates a backup plan that will keep everyone awake and slowly destroy dreaming itself.
20. **Everyone Woke at a Different Dawn** — Unsynchronized waking strands families in incompatible versions of morning.
21. **The Perfect Morning Wanted to Stay** — A district knowingly chooses a beautiful dream, forcing Amaya to respect an informed decision she dislikes.
22. **Ishir Heard the Perfect Song** — Ishir must choose between waking with uncertainty and remaining where his music is complete.
23. **Some Dreams Could Not Go Home** — The team confronts fragments whose dreamers died, changed identity or explicitly refused return.
24. **Her Happiest Memory Asked to Live** — A dream-copy of young Amaya refuses storage, claiming the right to become a person like Moth.
25. **The City Put Dreams on Trial** — A public hearing must distinguish dangerous fragments from emerging persons without letting one authority decide.
26. **Dev Tried to Carry Every Nightmare** — Dev's buried failsafe turns remorse into a citywide forced collection.
27. **Amaya Could Steal One Last Dream** — The compass offers Amaya one irreversible extraction: erase the city's memory of dream theft itself.
28. **The Nightmares Returned by Choice** — Saanjh begins the careful return of fear, one dreamer-controlled fragment at a time.
29. **Amaya Opened Her Own Nightmare** — Amaya faces the fragment she postponed and discovers fear of becoming Dev beneath fear of death.
30. **The Dream Thief's Daughter Slept** — Amaya builds a future defined neither by her father's crime nor by magical exceptionalism.
