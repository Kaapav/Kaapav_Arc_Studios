# ECHO//100 — Episode 1 Studio Approval Draft

Status: CREATIVE APPROVAL ONLY — do not render, upload, or publish.

## Packaging

- Working title: **The Dead Phone Rang at 2:17 AM | ECHO//100 Ep. 1**
- Target runtime: **42 seconds**
- Format: **9:16 Short, 24 fps delivery**
- Genre: **science-fiction mystery / thriller**
- Story function: establish Kavi, Byte, Mira, Null, the dead phone, and the Red Door; end on a hard consequence.
- Visual rule: every shot is generated motion video. No Ken Burns movement, slideshow holds, stock footage, or recycled shot used as a new event.

## Locked character identity

- Kavi: South Asian teenager, round glasses, red hoodie, blue backpack.
- Byte: small white floating robot, black face screen, cyan eyes, orange antenna.
- Mira: controlled cyan holographic archive intelligence; concise, precise, never comic relief.
- Null: black-purple corrupted silhouette with red eyes; threatening, non-graphic, never fully explained in Episode 1.

## Audio identity

- No wall-to-wall generic music. Begin with electrical room tone and one impossible phone ring.
- Motif: three-note glass-and-synth signal for the phone; distorted inversion for Null.
- Give Byte a physical hover motor, tiny servo reactions, and a short power-drop sound.
- Dialogue uses three clearly distinct voices: future Kavi, Mira, and Byte. Narrator only bridges actions the image cannot communicate.
- End sound must loop naturally into the opening phone ring without repeating the picture.

## Approval gates before monthly batch

- Kavi and Byte remain recognizably identical across all eight shots.
- Every shot contains purposeful subject motion, not only camera motion.
- No hand, face, glasses, costume, robot, or doorway morph survives QC.
- Dialogue is intelligible on a phone speaker; music never masks the mystery clues.
- The final six seconds create a stronger unanswered consequence than the opening question.
- Episode feels authored as one scene with escalation, not eight unrelated model demonstrations.

## Explicitly rejected for this version

- “Pixar style” or imitation of any named studio/franchise.
- AI-generated text inside phone or arcade screens.
- Reusing the same reference frame for multiple supposed locations.
- Eight equal, slow five-second shots with identical push-ins.
- Raw concatenation into the Episode 1–10 compilation.
