# ECHO//100 — V2 Series Bible

## Production decision

This is a complete story rewrite, not a polish of the existing episodes. The old renders remain untouched for comparison. V2 is produced first as one continuous 25–30 minute film. Thirty connected 45–60 second Shorts are then extracted from the completed film; they are not separate remakes. Episode 30 closes the story without a sequel tease.

## The promise

Seventeen years ago, Navapur lost exactly 100 minutes. Every clock and recording jumps from 2:17 AM to 3:57 AM, and the city remembers only an electrical fire at Central Station.

At 2:17 AM, sixteen-year-old Kavi Rao's dead phone receives a voice note in his own voice: “At 3:57, Tara will be gone. Don't trust Byte.” A red maintenance door then appears inside an abandoned arcade. Behind it is the city that lived through those missing 100 minutes—and the people Navapur forgot.

ECHO//100 is ultimately about memory as responsibility. The solution is not another reset, a bigger weapon, or a chosen hero. The city survives only when ordinary people freely agree to remember a painful truth together.

## World rules

1. The Missing Hour lasts 100 minutes: 2:17–3:57 AM.
2. A Red Door can open for 100 seconds when powered by Byte's core.
3. Every crossing requires an exchange. A person, object, or memory entering or leaving must be balanced by something of equal emotional weight.
4. The dead phone carries voice notes exactly 100 minutes forward or backward along Kavi's personal timeline. It cannot predict arbitrary futures.
5. Rewriting or suppressing a memory does not destroy it. The rejected memory becomes part of Null.
6. Null grows when people deny the Missing Hour and becomes an individual when people remember it.
7. Byte can lock the fracture, but only by surrendering his accumulated memories. He cannot be ordered to make that sacrifice in V2; the decision must be his.
8. No late episode may introduce a new rule that solves the plot.

## Core cast

### Kavi Rao, 16

- Visual lock: South Asian boy, red hoodie, round glasses, blue repair backpack.
- Skill: Repairs obsolete electronics and notices mechanical patterns.
- Want: Prove his missing father did not abandon the family.
- Need: Stop treating people and grief as problems he can fix alone.
- Flaw: Hides dangerous information when he fears someone will stop him.
- Voice: Quick, exact, defensive humor; becomes quiet when frightened.
- Arc: Lone fixer → manipulative secret-keeper → accountable collaborator.

### Tara Malik, 16

- Visual lock: South Asian girl, mustard rain jacket, dark braid, large red headphones, portable cassette recorder.
- Skill: Pirate-radio host, interviewer, and fearless improviser.
- Want: Prove that the voice on an impossible cassette belongs to her erased brother Imran.
- Need: Accept that truth is not hers to expose without consent.
- Flaw: Broadcasts first and considers consequences later.
- Voice: Direct, funny, emotionally perceptive; never speaks like an exposition device.
- Arc: Truth hunter → reckless broadcaster → ethical witness who lets the city choose.

### Byte

- Visual lock: Small white floating repair robot, cyan face display, orange antenna, scuffed left panel.
- Skill: Repairs machines, maps Red Door energy, and delivers painfully literal jokes.
- Want: Be treated as Kavi's friend rather than his father's tool.
- Need: Exercise free choice even when his choice hurts Kavi.
- Flaw: Obeys hidden safety protocols and then rationalizes the betrayal.
- Arc: Secret lock → distrusted tool → autonomous person who chooses his own sacrifice.

### Mira Sen, 17

- Visual lock: Blue holographic girl in a teal transit coat; a cracked silver station badge remains readable only as a symbol, never generated text.
- Truth: Not an AI. She is the compressed consciousness of a real teenager trapped during the Missing Hour.
- Want: Recover a physical life outside the archive.
- Need: Accept that survival cannot require erasing the other trapped passengers.
- Flaw: Edits what she tells people to keep them moving toward her preferred outcome.
- Voice: Controlled, dry, observant; emotion breaks through as visual signal noise.
- Arc: Guide who withholds truth → compromised survivor → honest leader who risks remaining behind.

### Dr. Rhea Rao, 43

- Visual lock: South Asian transit engineer, silver-streaked dark hair, navy work coat, brass ECHO key on a cord.
- Skill: Designed Central Station's temporal prediction system.
- Want: Keep Kavi outside the catastrophe that took his father.
- Need: Admit that secrecy continued the harm she once contained.
- Flaw: Makes unilateral sacrifices and calls them protection.
- Arc: Obstructive parent → accountable architect → collaborator who accepts public consequences.

### Null

- Visual lock: Black-purple human silhouette made from layered faces and broken memories; soft red eyes, frightening but never monstrous or gory.
- Truth: The composite consciousness of 412 people and memories removed to stabilize Navapur.
- Want: Stop being used as the city's invisible landfill.
- Need: Become one accountable voice without owning everyone else's lives.
- Flaw: Forces people to experience pain because asking never worked.
- Voice: Starts as overlapping whispers; gradually resolves into a singular calm voice.
- Arc: Apparent predator → injured collective → recognized person and ally.

### Arvind Rao, 45 inside the Missing Hour

- Visual lock: South Asian engineer, worn maroon station sweater, rectangular glasses, grease-stained hands.
- Skill: Built Byte and the physical Red Door lock.
- Want: Prevent Kavi from inheriting his mistake.
- Need: Allow Kavi to make an informed choice rather than another protective lie.
- Function: Emotional answer to Kavi's abandonment wound, not a magical solver.

### Imran Malik, 35 inside the Missing Hour

- Visual lock: South Asian man, faded mustard radio jacket, Tara's matching red headphone cable tied around his wrist.
- Truth: Tara's older brother, trapped at eighteen and aged seventeen years inside repeating time.
- Function: Makes the exchange rule personal and gives Tara a choice she cannot solve with exposure.

## Season architecture

- Episodes 1–10: The door opens. The mystery becomes personal, then traps Kavi and Tara inside the Missing Hour.
- Episodes 11–20: The forgotten city. Every major character's lie is exposed; Navapur begins remembering.
- Episodes 21–30: The warning is the loop. The cast distributes the memory instead of choosing another victim, then closes the causal system.

## Anti-slop writing gate

Every episode must pass all of these before rendering:

- One character wants something specific before the plot interrupts.
- At least two characters disagree for understandable reasons.
- A choice—not a random reveal—causes the episode's consequence.
- No recap opening, generic prophecy, or unexplained power upgrade.
- No character changes loyalty without an established motive.
- A reveal answers an existing question and creates a harder decision.
- Dialogue carries personality; narration is used only for compression.
- The episode changes a relationship or permanent story state.
- The cliffhanger follows causally from the episode and is not merely a new glowing object.
- Eight distinct shot intentions; no four-image repetition across eight scenes.
- Character references are locked before images are generated.
- Generated visuals contain no text, logos, watermarks, extra characters, or imitation-studio styling.

## Visual and sound direction

- Original premium stylized 3D animation; tactile materials and restrained facial acting.
- Navapur combines rain-dark concrete, aging transit architecture, warm repair shops, and cyan/red temporal light.
- Red indicates an active exchange, cyan indicates recorded memory, purple indicates denied memory, warm amber indicates chosen human connection.
- Each episode requires at least eight compositionally distinct story images or native video shots.
- Camera motion must follow intent: pursuit, hesitation, concealment, recognition, or release. Decorative zooming does not count.
- Give every main character a sound motif. Byte's motif becomes less mechanical as he gains agency; Null's chorus resolves into one voice as the city remembers.

## Mandatory production order

1. Lock the complete screenplay and scene inventory for the long film.
2. Create a turnaround sheet for every recurring character: front, front three-quarter, profile, back three-quarter, back, plus expression references.
3. Approve the character sheets as immutable identity references.
4. Create location and prop reference sheets.
5. Generate story frames and native motion only from the locked references.
6. Assemble, sound-design, subtitle, and QC the 25–30 minute film.
7. Extract the 30 Shorts from the approved film and adapt only their openings/end cards where necessary.

No story image may be generated before the required character sheets exist. No Kaggle workflow is part of V2.

## Ending contract

The Missing Hour becomes public history. Mira and Imran return without another person being traded. Null is recognized instead of deleted. Rhea and Arvind face the consequences of Project ECHO. Byte survives but voluntarily gives up his memories. Kavi does not retain a secret superpower. At the final 2:17 AM, nothing rings.
